Research Publications

Working papers written with my co-authors at UC Berkeley, advised by Prof. Ali Kakhbod. Most recent first.

  • NoLBERT-Calls: Reading Earnings Calls Without Hindsight

    Kevin Foley, Jonathan Hartadi, Shivesh Prakash, Hino Wirachapong Suwanphibun, Swapnil Vatsal

    arXiv:2611.19849, November 2026

    Language models for earnings calls are usually pretrained on text from the same years used for backtesting, so a holdout period does not remove look-ahead bias. We adapt NoLBERT, an encoder pretrained only on 1976 to 1995 text, to earnings calls using data through 2023, training it to match each management answer to the analyst question it follows. On 20,323 US calls from January 2025 to October 2026, it ranks the correct answer first for 78.0% of questions, against 26.3% before adaptation, and a year-by-year test finds no look-ahead advantage.

    arXivSSRN

  • Reading the News Without Hindsight: NoLBERT Topics and the Cross-Section of Stock Returns

    Kevin Foley, Jonathan Hartadi, Shivesh Prakash, Charishma Takkallapalli, Swapnil Vatsal

    arXiv:2610.22745, October 2026

    We build news topics with NoLBERT, which never saw text from the test period, and compare it with LDA, MiniLM and FinBERT on 1.26 million articles from 2012 to 2024. Each reader's topics become attention shocks and stock exposures used to forecast returns of the 1,000 largest US stocks, with 2022 to 2024 held out. NoLBERT gives the most stable topics, and an equal-weight combination of all readers and models earns a monthly net Sharpe ratio of 1.12, significant against zero but not against a price-only benchmark.

    arXivSSRN

  • From Word Counts to Context: Topic Models for Asset Pricing

    Kevin Foley, Jonathan Hartadi, Shivesh Prakash, Swapnil Vatsal

    arXiv:2609.34169, September 2026

    We test whether sentence-transformer topics improve on Latent Dirichlet Allocation for building news-driven risk factors, running both through the same portfolio pipeline on 394,661 articles. The transformer topics are more coherent and post higher Sharpe ratios, though our tests do not confirm a clear win over LDA, and exploratory variants reach a combined excess-return Sharpe of 1.03.
